Christmas baby names with meanings

Updated 24 November 2026

Expecting a baby around Christmas, or just love the season? These names carry a little festive magic without feeling like a costume. Some mean “Christmas” outright, some come from the evergreens we bring indoors, and others go back to the Nativity story.

Tap any name to read its full meaning and origin.

Names that mean Christmas

  • Noel (Girl or boy): The English form of the French Noël, meaning “Christmas”. The musician Noel Gallagher is a famous bearer.
  • Noelle (Girl): The English form of Noëlle, also meaning “Christmas”. A softer choice for a girl.
  • Natalie (Girl): From the Late Latin Natalia, meaning “Christmas Day”. The actress Natalie Wood made it popular.
  • Natalia (Girl): The Latin form of Natalie, with the same meaning: “Christmas Day”.
  • Christmas (Girl or boy): Straight from the name of the holiday, for parents who want no doubt at all.

Evergreen names

Holly and ivy stay green all winter, which is why they became Christmas decorations.

  • Holly (Girl): From the evergreen holly tree. Holly Golightly is the heroine of “Breakfast at Tiffany’s”.
  • Ivy (Girl): From the climbing evergreen plant. Short, sweet and very popular.
  • Hollis (Girl or boy): From a surname meaning “holly trees”. A quieter way to get the holly in.

Nativity and angel names

  • Mary (Girl): The mother of Jesus. For centuries it has been the most popular girls’ name in the Christian world.
  • Joseph (Boy): From the Hebrew Yosef, “he shall add”. Saint Joseph was the husband of Mary.
  • Gabriel (Boy): Hebrew for “God is my strength”. Gabriel is the archangel who announces the birth of Jesus.
  • Emmanuel (Boy): From Hebrew, meaning “God is with us”, a name for the Messiah in the Old Testament.
  • Jasper (Boy): Traditionally the name of one of the three wise men. It is also a gemstone.
  • Angel (Girl or boy): From the Greek word for “messenger”. In English it is often used for girls.
  • Seraphina (Girl): From “seraphim”, meaning “fiery ones”, the highest order of angels.
  • Gloria (Girl): Latin for “glory”, from Spanish and Portuguese titles of the Virgin Mary.
  • Celeste (Girl or boy): From the Latin word for “heavenly”, used for both boys and girls.
  • Evangeline (Girl): From Greek, meaning “good news”. First used by Longfellow in his 1847 poem.

Festive spirit

  • Merry (Girl): From the English word “merry”. Christmas is right there in the name.
  • Joy (Girl): From the English word “joy”, used as a name since the 19th century.
  • Carol (Girl or boy): A short form of Caroline, and also the word for a festive song.
  • Star (Girl): From the English word “star”. Simple and bright.
  • Stella (Girl): Latin for “star”, first used as a name by the poet Philip Sidney.
  • Nicholas (Boy): Greek for “victory of the people”. Saint Nicholas, known for secret gift-giving, inspired Santa Claus.
  • Rudolph (Boy): The English form of Rudolf, made famous by Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer.

Cosy winter names

  • Snow (Girl): From the English word “snow”. Perfect for a white Christmas baby.
  • Winter (Girl): From the name of the season.
  • Robin (Girl or boy): Made famous by Robin Hood and linked with the songbird. Now used for boys and girls.
  • Eve (Girl): From Hebrew, related to words meaning “to breathe” or “to live”. A sweet nod to Christmas Eve.
