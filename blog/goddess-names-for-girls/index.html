Goddess names for girls

Updated 24 November 2026

Want a name with a bit of power behind it? These come from the goddesses of Greek, Roman, Norse and Celtic myth. Some are as famous as Athena, others hide a goddess in their meaning, like June (from Juno) or Cora (another name of Persephone).

Tap any name to read its full meaning and origin.

Greek and Roman goddesses

  • Athena (Girl): Goddess of wisdom and war, and protector of Athens.
  • Diana (Girl): Latin for “divine”. Roman goddess of the moon and hunting, and the name of Princess Diana.
  • Aurora (Girl): The Roman goddess who brought each new morning. The northern lights share her name.
  • Minerva (Girl): The Roman goddess of wisdom.
  • Luna (Girl): Latin for “moon”. In Roman belief Luna was the moon goddess.
  • Selena (Girl): From Selene, the Greek goddess of the moon.
  • Iris (Girl): Greek for “rainbow”. The messenger of the gods and goddess of the rainbow.
  • Flora (Girl): From Latin for “flower”. The Roman goddess of blossoms and springtime.
  • Irene (Girl): Greek for “peace”. Eirene was the Greek goddess of peace.

Norse and Celtic goddesses

  • Freya (Girl): Old Norse for “lady”. The Norse goddess of love and beauty.
  • Bridget (Girl): From the Irish Brighid, “the exalted one”, the name of an Irish goddess.

Names that secretly honour a goddess

  • Cora (Girl): From Kore, another name of the Greek goddess Persephone.
  • Cynthia (Girl): “Woman from Cynthus”, the mountain where Artemis was born.
  • Delia (Girl): Greek for “from Delos”, the island where Artemis was born.
  • Chloe (Girl): Greek for “green shoot”, an epithet of the goddess Demeter.
  • Alexandra (Girl): The feminine form of Alexander, and an epithet of the goddess Hera.
  • June (Girl): From the month, named after the Roman goddess Juno.
  • May (Girl): From the month, named after the Roman goddess Maia.

Nymphs, Titans and heroines

  • Daphne (Girl): Greek for “laurel”. The nymph Daphne escaped Apollo by turning into a laurel tree.
  • Phoebe (Girl): Greek for “bright” or “pure”. In myth Phoebe was a Titan linked with the moon.
  • Melissa (Girl): Greek for “bee”. The nymph who cared for the infant Zeus.
  • Ione (Girl): Greek for “violet”. In myth Ione was a sea nymph.
  • Cassandra (Girl): She could see the future, but was cursed so that no one believed her.
  • Penelope (Girl): In Homer’s “Odyssey”, the faithful wife of Odysseus.
  • Helen (Girl): Possibly “torch”. Helen of Troy’s beauty started the Trojan War.
  • Rhiannon (Girl): Probably “great queen”. In Welsh legend Rhiannon rides a white horse.

Mythology names for boys

Gods and heroes, for a little brother or if you’re having a boy.

  • Odin (Boy): From a word meaning “inspiration” or “frenzy”. Odin is the chief of the Norse gods.
  • Jason (Boy): Greek for “healer”. Jason led the Argonauts in search of the Golden Fleece.
  • Arthur (Boy): King Arthur, the legendary ruler of Camelot.
  • Tristan (Boy): Tristan’s tragic love for Iseult is one of the great medieval romances.
  • Wayland (Boy): The legendary master smith of Germanic myth.
