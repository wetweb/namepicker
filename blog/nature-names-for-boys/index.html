Nature names for boys

Updated 24 November 2026

Nature names aren’t just for girls. These boys’ names come from forests, wild animals, rocks and water. Some say it outright, like Bear and Stone. Others hide it in an old meaning, like Silas (“of the forest”) or Orson (“bear cub”).

Tap any name to read its full meaning and origin.

Trees and forests

  • Forrest (Boy): From a surname for someone who lived near a forest. The film “Forrest Gump” made it famous.
  • Alder (Boy): From the English name of the alder tree.
  • Silas (Boy): Probably a short form of Silvanus, meaning “of the forest”. Silas is a companion of Paul in the New Testament.
  • Elwood (Boy): From a surname meaning “elder tree forest”.

Wild animals

  • Leo (Boy): Latin for “lion”. Borne by thirteen popes and by the novelist Leo Tolstoy.
  • Leon (Boy): Greek for “lion”, long used interchangeably with Leo.
  • Bear (Boy): From the English word for the animal. Big, soft and strong.
  • Orson (Boy): From a Norman nickname meaning “bear cub”, borne by the director Orson Welles.
  • Bernard (Boy): From Germanic, meaning “strong as a bear”.
  • Wolf (Boy): A short form of Wolfgang and similar names, or simply from the animal.
  • Lowell (Boy): From a surname meaning “little wolf”.
  • Ulric (Boy): A medieval form of Wulfric, meaning “wolf ruler”.
  • Fox (Boy): From the English word for the animal, or from the surname Fox.
  • Corbin (Boy): From a French surname meaning “raven”.
  • Colt (Boy): From the word for a young male horse.

Rocks, hills and valleys

  • Flint (Boy): From the hard stone used to make sparks.
  • Stone (Boy): From the English word “stone”.
  • Peter (Boy): From the Greek word for “stone”. Jesus gave the name to the apostle Simon.
  • Craig (Boy): From a Scottish surname meaning “crag” or “rock”.
  • Ridge (Boy): From the English word for a mountain crest.
  • Glenn (Boy): From a Scottish surname meaning “valley”.
  • Heath (Boy): From a surname for someone who lived on a heath.
  • Clay (Boy): From a surname for someone who lived near clay soil, or a short form of Clayton.

Water and wild places

  • Dylan (Boy): From Welsh, perhaps meaning “great tide”. In Welsh legend Dylan is a sea figure.
  • Kai (Boy): Of uncertain origin, and it also matches the Hawaiian word for “sea”.
  • Ford (Boy): From a surname for someone who lived by a river crossing.
  • Wade (Boy): From a surname meaning “ford” or “river crossing”.
  • Lyle (Boy): From a surname meaning “island”.
  • Wilder (Boy): From a surname meaning “wild”. For a little adventurer.

Nature names for boys or girls

These work for any baby. Handy if you don’t know yet, or just love a name that isn’t tied to one gender.

  • Rowan (Girl or boy): A traditional Irish name meaning “little red one”, also linked to the rowan tree.
  • River (Girl or boy): From the English word for a flowing body of water.
  • Cedar (Girl or boy): From the name of the evergreen tree.
  • Ash (Girl or boy): A short form of Ashley, or simply from the ash tree.
  • Oakley (Girl or boy): From a surname meaning “oak clearing”.
  • Linden (Girl or boy): From a surname meaning “linden tree”.
  • Brook (Girl or boy): From a surname for someone who lived by a brook.
  • Storm (Girl or boy): From the English word “storm”.
